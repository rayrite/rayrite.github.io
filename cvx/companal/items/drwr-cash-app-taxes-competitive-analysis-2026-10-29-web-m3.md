# Competitive Analysis — Cash App Taxes vs. TaxHawk vs. FreeTaxUSA

**Target product:** Cash App Taxes (Cash App Taxes, Inc., a Block, Inc. company)
**Comparison set named by requester:** Cash App Taxes · TaxHawk · FreeTaxUSA
**Report date:** Thursday, 29 October 2026
**Market:** US consumer DIY tax preparation & e-filing (individual Form 1040 filers)

---

## 1. Header & Market Framing

Cash App Taxes is Block, Inc.'s free consumer tax-filing product, operated inside the Cash App wallet app. It prepares and e-files federal and state individual returns at $0 for the entire supported form set, with no paid tiers, no paid add-ons and no paywall gates — a structural break from an industry that prices by return complexity. It competes in the **US consumer DIY tax preparation and e-filing** category: software-only preparation of an individual Form 1040 (plus state equivalents), normally priced per federal return plus a per-state fee, with human-expert assistance sold separately as an upsell. The buyer is a US filer who wants to e-file without a CPA, typically in the free-tier-seeking price bracket. As of this report's date, the 2026 filing season (tax year 2025) has closed — the federal deadline was 15 April 2026 and the extended deadline was 15 October 2026 — so the live competitive action is now in **off-season product announcements for the tax year 2026 season that opens January 2027** ([IRS IR-2026-05](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/use-irs-free-file-to-conveniently-file-your-return-at-no-cost), [IRS IR-2026-101](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-reminder-extension-filers-can-use-irs-free-file-this-summer)).

> **Assumption stated up front.** The requester named three products but did not name a single target. Because the matrix format of this skill requires one target with named competitors, **Cash App Taxes is treated as the target** — it is the market leader in the free-federal-and-state segment, the most recently product-relevant of the three, and the only one of the three that is a standalone competitive threat. TaxHawk and FreeTaxUSA are treated as direct competitors, and the recency sweep (Section 3) added the competitors a 2026-era analysis cannot ignore. Section 3.1 records a structural finding that materially changes how the three named products relate to one another: **two of the three named products are the same company and the same software engine.**

## 3. Market Overview

### 3.1 The structural finding: two of the three named products are one product

**FreeTaxUSA and TaxHawk are not competitors. They are two brands of one company running one software engine.**

- FreeTaxUSA's own privacy policy states: "FreeTaxUSA is owned and operated by TaxHawk, Inc." — © 2002-2026 FreeTaxUSA, a TaxHawk, Inc. owned and operated website ([FreeTaxUSA privacy policy, updated 27 Aug 2026](https://auth.freetaxusa.com/about)). The privacy policy names TaxHawk's address as 1255 S State St, Provo, UT 84606.
- TaxHawk, Inc.'s own LinkedIn company page states the company "owns and operates three tax preparation websites: freetaxusa.com, taxhawk.com, and express1040.com" ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/taxhawk-inc)).
- The IRS published a news release in 2015 describing "TaxHawk, Inc., the owner and operator of FreeTaxUSA.com" ([IRS PDF, Oct 2015](https://www.irs.gov/pub/newsroom/Tax_Hawk_news_release_October_2015.pdf)); a second 2015 IRS release does the same ([IRS PDF](https://www.irs.gov/pub/newsroom/Tax%20Hawk%20news%20release%20on%202015%20Security%20Summit.pdf)).
- Both brands publish machine-readable `llms.txt` summaries with near-identical bodies, including identical pricing, identical feature enumerations, identical 82M+ cumulative returns filed, identical 4.8/5 rating from 23K+ reviews, and an identical claimed current tax year of 2025 with an IRS filing deadline of 15 April 2026 ([taxhawk.com/llms.txt](https://www.taxhawk.com/llms.txt), [freetaxusa.com/llms.txt](https://www.freetaxusa.com/llms.txt)).
- Independent analysis reaches the same conclusion: "They have not [diverged]. TaxHawk and FreeTaxUSA are both operated by TaxHawk, Inc. They run on the same underlying platform, use the same tax engine, and present essentially the same filing experience under two brands… The same tax calculation engine. The same interview flow and interface design. The same forms coverage, including Schedule C, Schedule E, Schedule D, and K-1s" ([Can I Deduct This](https://canideductthis.com/taxhawk-vs-freetaxusa/)). The same source adds: "Enter the same return in both and you get the same refund, because it is the same software computing the same law."
- The third brand, **Express1040** (express1040.com), runs on the same engine — its own sign-in portal reproduces TaxHawk's terms of service verbatim ("Via the Website, TaxHawk provides its Users with access to a variety of tax filing services…"), and its Deluxe page carries identical $7.99 / $64.99 tier pricing ([Express1040 sign-in / ToS](https://auth.express1040.com/), [Express1040 Deluxe](https://www.express1040.com/deluxe/)).

**Consequence for the requester's comparison:** choosing between TaxHawk and FreeTaxUSA is not a product decision. It is a brand-recognition and marketing decision, or occasionally a decision about which brand is running a promotion on the day you file. The real comparison is **Block's free product vs. TaxHawk, Inc.'s free product** — and that reduces almost entirely to one axis: **Cash App Taxes charges $0 for state; TaxHawk, Inc. charges $15.99 per state.** Everything else about the two offerings is functionally equivalent, with TaxHawk, Inc. holding decisive advantages on support, imports, prior-year filing and amended returns.

### 3.2 Who buys, what problem it solves, and how the market is currently shaped

**The buyer.** US individual filers who want to e-file without a CPA. The segment splits sharply into two camps: those whose return is simple enough to fit a gated free tier (roughly 37% of filers qualify for TurboTax Free Edition, roughly 52% for H&R Block Free Online), and those whose return is complex enough that every gated free tier rejects them. The second camp — self-employed, investors, landlords, multi-form filers — is where the "free federal regardless of complexity" model has taken over.

**The pricing collapse.** The category's free tier has bifurcated into two models:

- **Model A — free federal at any complexity, paid state, paid support.** TaxHawk Inc. ($0 federal / $15.99 state), Credit Karma Tax ($0 federal / $15 state), Cash App Taxes ($0 federal / $0 state).
- **Model B — free tier gated to simple returns, complexity forces a paid tier.** TurboTax (Free Edition $0 + $0 state, simple 1040 only, ~37% eligible; Deluxe $79; Premium $139), H&R Block (Free Online $0 + $0 state, ~52% eligible; Deluxe $65; Premium $105; Self-Employed $130), TaxAct (Free $0 + $54.99 state; paid tiers $59.99–$109.99), TaxSlayer (Simply Free $0 with narrow eligibility and state included only "at certain times during the tax filing season"; Classic $44.99 + $47.99 state).

Model A is winning the complex-return segment that Model B refuses. Cash App Taxes' own positioning leans on exactly this: "no matter your tax situation, you can file your federal and state taxes for free… even if you sold stocks or bitcoin" ([cash.app/taxes](https://cash.app/taxes)).

**The government channel has consolidated around IRS Free File.** The IRS killed Direct File in October 2025, leaving IRS Free File as the flagship free channel with an AGI threshold of **$89,000 for tax year 2025** and **eight private partners** for 2026: 1040Now, Drake (1040.com), ezTaxReturn.com, FileYourTaxes.com, On-Line Taxes, TaxAct, **TaxHawk (FreeTaxUSA)**, and TaxSlayer ([IRS IR-2026-05, 9 Jan 2026](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/use-irs-free-file-to-conveniently-file-your-return-at-no-cost); [IRS IR-2026-38, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/free-options-and-resources-for-preparing-and-filing-taxes-in-2026)). Section 70607 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act appropriated $15M for a Treasury study of public-private free-filing models, and the resulting task force is to evaluate partnership options that could reach **up to 70% of all taxpayers by AGI** ([CRS IF13314](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13314/IF13314.3.pdf); [Instead](https://www.instead.com/resources/blog/irs-direct-file-is-gone-for-2026-and-how-to-save-more)). **Cash App Taxes is not an IRS Free File partner.** That is a structural and permanent distribution ceiling on the target product, and it is the single most consequential asymmetry in this market.

**The 2026 law change that reset the table.** Schedule 1-A, created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, consolidates four new deductions — no tax on tips (up to $25,000), no tax on overtime (up to $12,500; $25,000 joint), no tax on car loan interest, and an enhanced seniors' deduction (up to $6,000 per person) — and is filed with Form 1040 for tax year 2025 returns ([IRS FS-2026-04, Mar 2026](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/schedule-1-a-additional-deductions-what-to-know-about-the-new-form); [IRS IR-2026-28, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-published-schedule-taxpayers-will-use-to-claim-deductions-on-no-tax-on-tips-no-tax-on-overtime-no-tax-on-car-loans-no-tax-on-seniors)). H&R Block shipped Schedule 1-A across all tiers including the free edition with auto-detection during the interview ([Axis Intelligence](https://axis-intelligence.com/best-tax-software/)); TurboTax Free Edition explicitly includes Schedule 1-A among the forms its $0 tier covers ([TurboTax](https://turbotax.intuit.com/personal-taxes/online/deluxe.jsp)). PCMag named Schedule 1-A handling as the reason it preferred TurboTax over Cash App Taxes in its 2026 free-software survey ([PCMag](https://uk.pcmag.com/tax-software/163215/the-best-free-tax-sofware-for-2026)).

### 3.3 The competitor that arrived nine days ago

**On 20 October 2026 — nine days before this report — Intuit launched Credit Karma Tax** ([Intuit press release #1328](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1328/intuit-launches-credit-karma-tax-free-federal-filing-with-faster-refunds-expanding-its-daily-ai-powered-financial-assistance); [Business Wire original](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261020041718/en/)).

The name is the story. **"Credit Karma Tax" is the exact product Block bought from Intuit for $50 million in cash at the end of 2020** — the DOJ's Antitrust Division required Intuit to divest the entire Credit Karma Tax business (software and IP) before allowing its $7.1B acquisition of Credit Karma to close, on the grounds that the deal would eliminate head-to-head competition in DIY tax prep ([DOJ consent decree analysis](https://legalclarity.org/credit-karma-lawsuit-ftc-settlement-class-action-and-more/); [Block company news, 8 Oct 2020](https://block.xyz/news/company-news)). The divestiture completed 8 December 2020 ([DOJ](https://legalclarity.org/credit-karma-lawsuit-ftc-settlement-class-action-and-more/)); Block relaunched it inside Cash App and renamed it Cash App Taxes on 16 October 2021 ([Cash App Taxes help centre](https://taxeshelp.cash.app/s/article/Getting-started-with-Cash-App-Taxes)).

Intuit has now reclaimed the brand and relaunched it as a **direct competitor to its own former divested asset**, operating "as a distinct, streamlined tax preparation offering" separate from TurboTax ([Intuit PR](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1328/intuit-launches-credit-karma-tax-free-federal-filing-with-faster-refunds-expanding-its-daily-ai-powered-financial-assistance)).

Intuit's Credit Karma Tax offers **$0 federal filing "no matter their tax complexity" and a flat $15 per state filing**, plus a very different feature stack from Cash App Taxes: photo-snapping and automatic import of forms directly from employers and financial institutions **including stock sales and financial and crypto transactions**; pre-filled returns; a comprehensive document checklist and gathering tools; a built-in AI expert chat with "Explanation and Error Agents" coming soon; **refunds up to 5 days early** free via a Credit Karma Checking account; and a **Refund Advance loan of up to $4,000 at $0 in fees and 0% APR** with its tax year 2026 offering. Two paid add-ons launched at $29 (Expert Support Bundle — audit defense, ID protection, 1:1 live product support, and the ability to pay the $15 state fee out of the refund) and $39 (Expert Final Review) ([Intuit PR](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1328/intuit-launches-credit-karma-tax-free-federal-filing-with-faster-refunds-expanding-its-daily-ai-powered-financial-assistance); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/10/20/intuit-offers-free-federal-tax-filing-with-credit-karma-tax/192063/)). Analysts immediately framed it as a price-sensitive-filer land grab: "The $0 federal and $15 state pricing gives Intuit a clearer entry point for price sensitive filers who previously churned from DIY tax products" ([Yahoo Finance](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/intuit-intu-rolls-free-federal-043035843.html)).

## 7. Strategic Observations

**7.1 — The requester's three-way comparison is really a two-company comparison, and the meaningful axis is $15.99.**
TaxHawk and FreeTaxUSA are one engine operated by one company, TaxHawk, Inc., along with a third brand, Express1040 ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/taxhawk-inc), [FreeTaxUSA privacy policy](https://auth.freetaxusa.com/about)). Their price sheets are identical line for line — $0 federal, $15.99 state, $7.99 Deluxe, $19.99 Audit Defense, $16.98 amendments, $17.99 prior-year state ([TaxHawk](https://www.taxhawk.com/about/), [FreeTaxUSA](https://www.freetaxusa.com/), [Express1040](https://www.express1040.com/software/)). The only non-product differences are brand recognition (FreeTaxUSA is the flagship and carries PCMag's 2026 Readers' Choice win), community size, and whatever promotion each brand is running that week. Cash App Taxes beats all three on one line item — **state filing at $0** — and loses to all three on support, imports, prior-year filing, amendments and entity coverage.

**7.2 — Cash App Taxes' competitive moat is one price point wide, and it is being narrowed from two sides.**
The product's entire structural advantage is that it charges $0 for federal *and* state with no tiers and no gates. TaxHawk, Inc. already matches it on "free federal at any complexity" and charges only $15.99 for state. Intuit's Credit Karma Tax launched on 20 October 2026 matched that same free-federal model at $15 per state and added AI agents, a refund advance loan and $29/$39 support bundles ([Intuit PR](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1328/intuit-launches-credit-karma-tax-free-federal-filing-with-faster-refunds-expanding-its-daily-ai-powered-financial-assistance)). Cash App Taxes' defensible lead is therefore down to the $15-per-state gap, plus whatever ecosystem lock-in a Cash App account creates.

**7.3 — Intuit is now fighting Block with a weapon Intuit built and Block bought.**
The Credit Karma Tax brand went Credit Karma → Intuit (2020 acquisition) → Block (Dec 2020 DOJ-mandated divestiture for $50M) → Cash App Taxes (Oct 2021 rename) → Intuit again (Oct 2026 relaunch) ([DOJ/Finovate summary](https://legalclarity.org/credit-karma-lawsuit-ftc-settlement-class-action-and-more/), [Cash App help centre](https://taxeshelp.cash.app/s/article/Getting-started-with-Cash-App-Taxes), [Intuit PR](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1328/intuit-launches-credit-karma-tax-free-federal-filing-with-faster-refunds-expanding-its-daily-ai-powered-financial-assistance)). Intuit now holds both TurboTax and Credit Karma Tax in the same Consumer segment ([Intuit 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm)) and can attack the $0-federal segment from two directions while owning the AI layer that the target does not have.

**7.4 — The target has not announced its tax year 2026 product while Intuit has.**
As of 19 October 2026, WealthyPot recorded that "Cash App Taxes had not announced its product for 2026 returns" and that "Prices for 2026 returns had not been published yet" across the whole category ([WealthyPot](https://wealthypot.com/tax-software/)). Intuit announced its TY2026 offering on 20 October. Since today's federal and extended deadlines have both passed (15 April and 15 October 2026), and IRS Free File opened for the 2025 tax year on 9 January 2026 ([IRS IR-2026-05](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/use-irs-free-file-to-conveniently-file-your-return-at-no-cost)), the TY2027 season opens in roughly mid-to-late January 2027. **A Cash App Taxes TY2026 announcement is now the single highest-value thing to watch.** In prior years Cash App Taxes shipped a "What's new" style release; the absence of one 8+ weeks before season open, in a year where the two former owners of the same brand are both shipping, is a meaningful signal.

**7.5 — IRS Free File is now the government-chosen free channel, and the target is locked out of it.**
For tax year 2025 the IRS Free File programme serves AGI ≤ $89,000 filers through eight partners — including TaxHawk (FreeTaxUSA), TaxAct and TaxSlayer — and **Cash App Taxes is not among them** ([IRS IR-2026-05](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/use-irs-free-file-to-conveniently-file-your-return-at-no-cost); [WealthyPot](https://wealthypot.com/tax-software/cash-app-taxes/)). Section 70607 of the OBBBA directs Treasury to study public-private free filing reaching up to 70% of taxpayers by AGI ([CRS IF13314](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13314/IF13314.3.pdf)). TaxHawk, Inc. — a $50M private company — holds a seat at that table through the Free File Alliance; Intuit holds two seats via TurboTax and Credit Karma Tax; Block holds none. In a market where the free-filing subsidy is being re-legislated, that is the target's most durable structural disadvantage.

**7.6 — Block's distribution advantage is real but under-monetised.**
Cash App reaches roughly 55–59 million monthly transacting users ([Q2 2026 earnings transcript](https://uk.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-block-lifts-2026-outlook-after-strong-q2-2026-93CH-4813203)), and Cash App Taxes has filed 12M+ returns all at $0 ([cash.app/taxes](https://cash.app/taxes)) against TaxHawk, Inc.'s 82M+ company-wide cumulative returns ([taxhawk.com/llms.txt](https://www.taxhawk.com/llms.txt)). The strategic gap is not reach — it is that Cash App Taxes has **no monetisation layer at all**: no paid support tier, no audit-defence upsell, no refund advance, no AI-assisted tier. Every dollar of TaxHawk, Inc.'s and Intuit's revenue in this category comes from precisely the layers Cash App Taxes does not sell ([TaxHawk pricing](https://www.taxhawk.com/about/); [Intuit PR](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1328/intuit-launches-credit-karma-tax-free-federal-filing-with-faster-refunds-expanding-its-daily-ai-powered-financial-assistance)). Cash App Taxes is a retention and engagement feature inside the wallet, not a P&L line — which is coherent, but it also means it will never out-invest a competitor that monetises the same user.

**7.7 — Two concrete, fixable churn leaks in the target.**
- **Prior-year filing is unsupported.** Cash App Taxes cannot file prior-year returns at all ([Cash App help centre](https://taxeshelp.cash.app/s/article/Getting-started-with-Cash-App-Taxes)), and its only amended-return path is print-and-mail, not e-filed ([WealthyPot](https://wealthypot.com/tax-software/cash-app-taxes/)). TaxHawk, Inc. files prior-year federal returns back to 2018 for **$0** and sells unlimited e-filed amendments for $16.98 ([TaxHawk llms.txt](https://www.taxhawk.com/llms.txt)). A Cash App Taxes user who discovers they need a prior-year amendment must leave the product entirely — and that is precisely the moment a filer evaluates a competitor.
- **Multi-state filing is unsupported**, including part-year and non-resident state returns and state-only filing ([Cash App help centre](https://taxeshelp.cash.app/s/article/Do-I-have-to-file-a-state-income-tax-return)). TaxHawk, Inc. charges $15.99 per state and accepts any number.

**7.8 — Document import is where the target is furthest behind.**
Cash App Taxes supports W-2 photo/PDF import but **not 1099 upload** — CNBC's 2026 review states plainly "1099 uploads are not supported yet" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/select/cash-app-taxes-review/)), and U.S. News notes it "requires manual entry for other forms like 1099s" ([U.S. News](https://money.usnews.com/personal-finance/tax-software/best-mobile-tax-apps)). Active crypto and stock traders must enter every trade by hand — FinanceBuzz warns this makes it "not the best choice" for active traders ([FinanceBuzz](https://financebuzz.com/credit-karma-tax-review)). Meanwhile TaxHawk, Inc. shipped consolidated 1099 import with brokerage PDF support in 2026 ([FreeTaxUSA 2026 changelog](https://community.freetaxusa.com/kb/articles/343-what-s-new-with-freetaxusa-in-2026)), TurboTax imports 1,500+ transactions from 300+ institutions and 2,000+ crypto transactions ([Financer](https://financer.com/review/turbotax/)), and Intuit's new Credit Karma Tax auto-imports from employers and financial institutions including crypto ([Intuit PR](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1328/intuit-launches-credit-karma-tax-free-federal-filing-with-faster-refunds-expanding-its-daily-ai-powered-financial-assistance)). **This is the one gap where the target is not merely cheaper but functionally behind — and it bites exactly in the segment (investors, gig workers) that a $0 price is designed to attract.**

**7.9 — White space the target could occupy without cannibalising anything.**
Nobody in the category currently offers "free federal *and* free state *and* a human expert at a flat, low price." TaxHawk, Inc. charges $64.99 for CPA/EA access; TurboTax's Expert Assist starts at $89; H&R Block's pro review is $55 and full service $99+; Intuit's new Expert Support Bundle is $29 and bundles audit defence, ID protection, live support and refund-paid state fees ([Intuit PR](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1328/intuit-launches-credit-karma-tax-free-federal-filing-with-faster-refunds-expanding-its-daily-ai-powered-financial-assistance)). A single **~$29–$39 Cash App Taxes support bundle** — the same price Intuit just chose — would put the target's human-help option at parity with its own free state filing and roughly half of every incumbent's expert tier. The target's block on this is that it has no expert supply chain; Intuit's is Intuit Assist + TurboTax experts, and TaxHawk, Inc.'s is an in-house CPA/EA bench powering Pro Support and ProCheck.

**7.10 — Parent-level context is a genuine risk factor, though not a tax-product one.**
Block's Cash App has absorbed federal and state enforcement over fraud handling and customer service: a CFPB order in January 2025 for up to $120M in consumer redress plus a $55M penalty, and a $45M settlement with 46 state attorneys general announced in July 2026 ([BestGuide](https://bestguide.com/review/cash-app-taxes/)). Neither action concerned tax preparation, but both bear on the brand trust that a tax product depends on. Block also cut roughly 40% of staff in 2025 betting on AI ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/block-stock-drops-11-after-earnings-miss-despite-mass-ai-layoffs/)). The relevant strategic point: **the single capability the target most conspicuously lacks versus Intuit's new entrant — AI assistance — is precisely the capability Block has been reorganising its entire company to build.** Block also has the strongest remaining consumer distribution asset in the category.

**7.11 — Where the target genuinely wins, and should keep winning.**
It is the only product in the matrix offering a genuinely $0 federal-and-state outcome with **no income limit, no complexity gate, no tier ladder and no paid add-on** ([cash.app/taxes](https://cash.app/taxes); [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/best-free-tax-software-2026/)), and the only one with **free audit defence** (Tax Protection Plus) plus a $1,000 accuracy guarantee and a $100 max-refund guarantee ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/select/cash-app-taxes-review/); [Forbes Advisor](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/taxes/cash-app-taxes-review/)). It also earns the only category-wide Readers' Choice recognition for state filing ([PCMag](https://www.pcmag.com/articles/readers-choice-2026-tax-apps-software)). That combination — free state, free audit defence, free guarantees — is the moat. Everything else in the target's product is currently behind at least one competitor.

**7.12 — Category-level structural observation.**
The OBBBA's Schedule 1-A turned tax-filing software into a rule-engine competition for four brand-new deductions with income phase-outs, and the IRS itself published implementation guidance because the mechanics were non-trivial ([IRS IR-2026-28](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-published-schedule-taxpayers-will-use-to-claim-deductions-on-no-tax-on-tips-no-tax-on-overtime-no-tax-on-car-loans-no-tax-on-seniors)). Early-season bugs in car-loan-interest phase-out calculations had to be patched across the category ([taxo.com](https://taxo.com/best-tax-software-2026-comparison/)). For a free product, a rule-engine error is disproportionately costly: there is no paid tier to absorb the liability, and the accuracy guarantee is the only remedy. That is a further argument for the target's most important near-term investment being **import and calculation accuracy**, not new features.
