### 7.1 Historical Precedents of Ticket-Splitting in Michigan
Michigan voters possess a long, documented tradition of ticket-splitting and independent voting:
- **2020 Presidential / Senate Cycle:** Democrat Gary Peters defeated Republican John James by **92,335 votes (49.9% to 48.2%)**, running approximately 60,000 votes behind Joe Biden's top-of-the-ticket margin in the state, demonstrating that several tens of thousands of suburban Michiganders split their tickets between a moderate presidential vote and a Republican Senate vote.
- **2024 Presidential / Senate Cycle:** In the razor-thin 2024 contest between Democrat Elissa Slotkin and Republican Mike Rogers, ticket-splitting was extremely compressed (under 0.8%), proving that in high-polarization environments, the presidential top-of-the-ticket and the Senate race track almost in direct parallel.

### 7.2 Can Mike Rogers Outperform Donald Trump?
For Mike Rogers to capture Michigan's Class II Senate seat, he must execute a "Trump-plus" performance:
1. **Retain the Entire MAGA Base:** Rogers cannot afford any erosion among rural, blue-collar, and populist voters who turn out specifically for Donald Trump. His full embrace of Trump’s endorsement and rally appearances secures this foundation.
2. **Outperform Trump in the Suburbs by 2–3 Points:** Because Trump trails in Michigan by 2 to 4 percentage points, Rogers must run ahead of Trump in suburban Oakland, Macomb, and Kent counties. To achieve this, Rogers consistently projects institutional seriousness, highlights his 20-year legislative record, emphasizes his bipartisan Benghazi report, and neutralizes Democratic abortion attacks by repeating his commitment to respect Proposal 3 and oppose a federal abortion ban.

### 7.3 Can Abdul El-Sayed Mobilize Without Alienating Moderates?
Conversely, for Abdul El-Sayed to secure victory, he must balance progressive enthusiasm with broad anti-Trump coalition maintenance:
1. **Maximize the Anti-Trump Turnout Engine:** El-Sayed's greatest asset is the intense antipathy toward Trump among college-educated suburbanites, young voters, and minority communities. By framing the election as a direct referendum on preventing a Trump-controlled MAGA Senate, he ensures maximum turnout.
2. **Insulate Against "Radical" Labeling:** Rogers' primary line of attack is branding El-Sayed's "Medicare for All" and Green New Deal platform as economically catastrophic for Michigan. El-Sayed must reassure moderate auto workers and suburban families that his progressive populism will lower their healthcare costs and protect their manufacturing jobs, rather than disrupt their private coverage.
