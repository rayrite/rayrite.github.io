## 1. Executive summary

### Directory Architecture and Purpose
This comprehensive research directory establishes an exhaustive, multi-tier operational inventory of digital election and voter-information infrastructure throughout the United States. Spanning the federal sovereign layer, all 50 states and the District of Columbia, and the top 53 metropolitan statistical areas (MSAs) and consolidated city-counties, this document maps the pathways through which American voters register, locate voting precincts, inspect sample ballots, verify qualifications, track cast ballots, and monitor campaign finance disclosures.

### Key Structural Takeaways
1. **Decentralized Bifurcation:** The United States exhibits a bifurcated voter administration model. While federal agencies (EAC, FEC, FVAP, DOJ) establish regulatory certifications, campaign disclosure frameworks, overseas voting protocols, and civil rights enforcement, state election divisions maintain the primary statutory voter rolls. However, actual ballot delivery, polling site operations, and initial vote canvassing remain fundamentally localized at the county or municipal clerk level.
2. **Online Voter Registration (OVR) Ubiquity and Remaining Gaps:** 44 states and the District of Columbia now operate functional Online Voter Registration (OVR) portals integrated with state motor vehicle agencies. Seven jurisdictions remain structural exceptions: North Dakota (which maintains no voter registration system by statute), and six states that require paper or in-person submission without standalone end-to-end web registration (Arkansas, Mississippi, Montana, New Hampshire, South Dakota, and Wyoming; Texas maintains limited DPS renewal-linked registration but lacks open web registration).
3. **Ballot Tracking Infrastructure:** Universal vote-by-mail (VBM) states (California, Colorado, Hawaii, Nevada, Oregon, Utah, Vermont, Washington, and Washington D.C.) have deployed comprehensive ballot-tracking ecosystems (e.g., BallotTrax, Ballot Scout, or bespoke state platforms like VoteWA and Cal-Access/Where's My Ballot?). In contrast, several non-excuse and excuse-required absentee states still lack centralized digital tracking, delegating verification to localized county portals or manual telephone inquiry.
4. **Voter Identification Polarization:** State voter identification statutes have diverged into five distinct legal categories. Thirteen states enforce strict photo ID statutes with mandatory provisional balloting unless cured (e.g., Georgia, Indiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, Ohio, Texas), ten states enforce non-strict photo ID laws with signed affidavit or secondary verification alternatives, twelve states permit non-photo documentary identification, and fourteen jurisdictions require no physical document at polling places for registered voters, relying on signature verification and personal identity declarations.
5. **Major Metro Governance Divergence:** In consolidated city-counties (e.g., New York City, San Francisco, Philadelphia, Denver, Honolulu), voter administration is unified under a single municipal board of elections or city clerk. In sprawling multi-county MSAs (e.g., Dallas-Fort Worth, Atlanta, Chicago, Washington D.C., Minneapolis-St. Paul), voters encounter fragmented jurisdictional boundaries where neighboring counties maintain disparate ballot request portals, voting equipment, and local campaign finance disclosure repositories.

### Top Nonpartisan Starting Points for Voters
For voters, researchers, and developers seeking immediate access to election logistics, five national nonpartisan portals provide vetted starting points:
- **Vote411.org (League of Women Voters):** The premier national nonpartisan guide for personalized, address-specific sample ballots and direct candidate questionnaires spanning federal, state, and down-ballot municipal contests.
- **Ballotpedia (Lucy Burns Institute):** The most exhaustive digital encyclopedia for federal, state, and local election coverage, providing candidate biographies, policy positions, ballot measure summaries, and court-drawn district maps.
- **Can I Vote / NASS (canivote.org):** The authoritative official redirect clearinghouse maintained by the National Association of Secretaries of State, routing users directly to their state's verified .gov registration status, polling place finder, and election results page.
- **Vote.org:** The leading high-throughput civic-tech platform for checking registration status, generating registration forms, and setting automated election reminders across all 50 states.
- **Vote Smart (justfacts.votesmart.org):** The definitive nonpartisan research database for candidate voting records, campaign finance summaries, interest group ratings, and issue stances.
