## 1. Matrix Methodology and Assessment Framework

In conformance with Section 7 and Section 16.D of the project specification, each of the 56 jurisdictions was independently investigated across **14 mandatory research topics**:

1. **T1: Chief Election Authority & Administrator Directory** (Secretary of State, State Election Board, County/Township Clerks).
2. **T2: Voter Registration, Polling-Place, Ballot & Calendar Portals** (My Voter Page, online registration, polling lookup).
3. **T3: Candidate & Ballot-Measure Information** (Official candidate filing lists, petition rules, certified ballot measures).
4. **T4: Official Results, Canvasses, Recounts & Audit Reports** (Historical returns, precinct returns, post-election audits).
5. **T5: Campaign Finance, Lobbying & Ethics Disclosures** (State electronic filing systems, donor search, lobbyist registries).
6. **T6: Legislative Portal & Developer/Bulk Data Access** (State legislative bill tracking, XML/JSON feeds, API availability).
7. **T7: Statutory Code, Session Laws, Rules & Executive Orders** (Codified statutes, state administrative registers, gubernatorial orders).
8. **T8: Supreme Court, Appellate, Trial Courts & Docket Systems** (Statewide court portals, public dockets, appellate opinions).
9. **T9: Judicial Selection, Discipline, Evaluation & Disclosures** (Judicial conduct commissions, merit selection panels, evaluation reports).
10. **T10: Attorney General & Prosecutor Directories / Associations** (State AG, Prosecuting Attorneys Associations, DA jurisdictions).
11. **T11: State Library, Archives & Historical Newspaper Repositories** (State archives, digital newspaper collections, FDLP hubs).
12. **T12: Statewide Public Media & Civic Watchdog Organizations** (State public radio networks, non-profit investigative newsrooms).
13. **T13: State GIS, Redistricting, Precinct & Boundary Resources** (State spatial data clearinghouses, redistricting shapefiles).
14. **T14: Public Records (FOIA) Procedures & Transparency Gaps** (State open records statutes, response timelines, commercial exemptions).

### Evaluative Status Codes
* `VRF` = **Verified Resource Found:** Authoritative primary resource or robust developer interface actively operational and verified.
* `PC` = **Partial Coverage:** Resource exists but exhibits material gaps in timeliness, local granularity, or historical depth.
* `RA` = **Restricted Access:** Dataset exists but is locked behind paywalls, subscription fees, or authorized credentials.
* `NPA` = **Not Publicly Available:** State law or administrative practice withholds data from open public release.
* `NF` = **Not Found After Documented Search:** Multi-pass search confirmed absence of an organized digital public resource.
* `NA` = **Not Applicable:** Structural or constitutional non-existence in that specific jurisdiction.

## 3. Jurisdiction-Specific Administrative Deep Dives

### 1. Divergent Local Election Governance Models
A fatal architectural mistake in civic application development is assuming that county governments always administer elections. The 56 jurisdictions fall into five distinct structural archetypes:

1. **Municipal / Township Administration (Upper Midwest & New England):**
   * **Michigan (`MI`):** Elections are administered by **1,520 local municipal clerks** (city, township, and village). County clerks serve canvassing, campaign finance, and election coordination functions, but voter registration and polling places belong exclusively to municipal clerks.
   * **Wisconsin (`WI`):** Elections are administered by **1,850 municipal clerks** under the supervision of the bipartisan Wisconsin Elections Commission (WEC).
   * **New England (CT, MA, ME, NH, VT, RI):** Town and city clerks administer elections directly; county government in New England is either abolished (CT, RI) or possesses minimal administrative power.
2. **Split County Administration (Arizona & Selected Western Counties):**
   * **Arizona (`AZ`):** Administration is structurally split between the elected **County Recorder** (responsible for voter registration, early voting, and mail ballots) and the appointed **County Elections Department / Board of Supervisors** (responsible for Election Day voting centers, equipment, tabulation, and canvassing).
3. **County Board of Elections Model (Mid-Atlantic & Southeast):**
   * **North Carolina (`NC`), Pennsylvania (`PA`), Georgia (`GA`), Ohio (`OH`):** Elections are administered by multi-member bipartisan or appointed county boards of elections (e.g., 5-member boards in NC, 4-member bipartisan pairs in OH, City Commissioners in Philadelphia).
4. **Unified Single-Tier State Administration (Alaska & Delaware):**
   * **Alaska (`AK`):** Has no county governments. The Division of Elections in the Office of the Lieutenant Governor administers all elections directly across five regional offices.
   * **Delaware (`DE`):** The state Department of Elections directly operates county-level branch offices.
5. **Territorial Governance & Non-Voting Federal Representation:**
   * **Puerto Rico (`PR`), Guam (`GU`), U.S. Virgin Islands (`VI`), American Samoa (`AS`), Northern Mariana Islands (`MP`):** Residents are U.S. citizens (or U.S. nationals in American Samoa) who elect non-voting Delegates or a Resident Commissioner to the U.S. House of Representatives. They do not have voting representation in the U.S. Senate or Electoral College votes for President in the general election, though they participate in presidential party primaries.

## 5. State FOIA / Open Records Statutory Architecture

When digital feeds or APIs are absent, developers must resort to state public records requests. The table below summarizes statutory timelines and commercial exemptions across key battleground jurisdictions:

| State | Public Records Statute | Statutory Response Deadline | Commercial Use Restrictions | Administrative Appeal Body |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AZ` | Arizona Public Records Law (A.R.S. § 39-121) | Promptly (no fixed business days) | Commercial requests must state purpose; fees apply | Superior Court civil action |
| `GA` | Georgia Open Records Act (O.C.G.A. § 50-18-70) | 3 business days | Reasonable search/copy fees; electronic format mandated | Georgia Attorney General Mediation |
| `MI` | Michigan Freedom of Information Act (MCL 15.231) | 5 business days (10-day extension) | Standard fees; no commercial prohibition | Circuit Court judicial appeal |
