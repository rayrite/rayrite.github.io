## Concept Statement

Three ordinary Detroiters. Three near-misses. One friend with a phone. A retro-electro laser sequence that obliterates the fraudster archetype. Detroit dancing in the streets with the money it kept. The Spirit of Detroit smiling quietly as ScamShield orbits the world in his hand.

This is not a pitch deck. This is a product placement spot built on the Detroit principle: *protect your people and let the work speak.*

### SCENE 8 — THE SPIRIT `1:12 – 1:27` 🏛️
**Location:** The Spirit of Detroit. Jefferson Avenue. Warm afternoon light on bronze.

## Production Notes

### On the Spirit of Detroit Sequence
The Spirit of Detroit is 65 years old. He has watched this city through everything. The subtlest possible animation is the most powerful choice. A 0.3% warp on the corner of the mouth in After Effects, held for 2 frames, is more powerful than any CGI rendering. If it looks like you moved it, you moved it too much.

### On the Laser Sequence Visual Style
Detroit techno was born in 1981 and it sounds like the future in 2026 because it was designed that way. The visual language for Scene 6 should feel like a direct descendant of that tradition: grid lines, neon cyan on black, amber accents, geometric precision. Chaos only in the explosion particles — everything else is clean.

### On Not Over-Explaining
The judges have read the submission materials. This video is not a product demo. It does not need to explain every feature. It needs to make someone *feel* what it would be like to have this tool in their pocket the moment they needed it. Scene 5 earns the product demo moment because scenes 3 and 4 earned it first.
